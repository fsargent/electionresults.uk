1. Introduction
This briefing provides a detailed overview of the results of
the Senedd general election held on 7 May 2026.

The Senedd and the 2026 election

The election of Members of the Senedd was held on Thursday 7 May 2026. The
election followed the dissolution of the Sixth Senedd at midnight on 8 April
2026. This was the seventh general election held for the Senedd and the first
under the new closed list proportional representation voting system. It was also
the first time that 96 Members were elected, rather than the 60 that had been
elected at elections between 1999-2021.

Counting of the votes took place on Friday 8 May, with the final declaration
made at 18:05 in Gwynedd Maldwyn.

Plaid Cymru won the largest share of the vote (35.4%) and the largest number of
seats (43), with Reform UK coming second with 29.3% of the vote and 34 seats.
Welsh Labour were in third place with 11.1% of the vote and 9 seats, followed by
the Welsh Conservatives who won 10.7% of the vote and 7 seats. Wales Green
Party were in fifth place with 6.7% of the vote and 2 seats. The Welsh Liberal
Democrats won 4.5% of the vote and 1 seat.

The turnout for the election was 51.7%, the highest there has ever been for a
Senedd general election.

Constituencies

Members of the Senedd were elected to represent 16 new Senedd
constituencies. Each was given a single Welsh language name by the
Democracy and Boundary Commission Cymru. The constituencies are set out in
the map below.

The electoral system

A closed list proportional representation electoral system was used for the first
time in Senedd elections. Under this system, registered political parties could
put forward lists of up to eight candidates in each constituency. Individual
candidates (independents) could also stand in a Senedd constituency.

The election was conducted across 16 constituencies, each electing six Members
of the Senedd. These constituencies were determined by the Democracy
and Boundary Commission Cymru by combining the 32 UK Parliament
constituencies into 16 Senedd constituencies.

After the counting of votes, the six seats in each constituency were allocated to
political parties and individual candidates using the D’Hondt method. More
information about the allocation of seats can be found in this Senedd
Research article.

Initial findings from the Welsh Election Study, led by researchers from
Aberystwyth University and Swansea University, showed that ahead of the
election, public understanding of the new electoral system was low. In total,
88% of respondents either answered incorrectly or did not know about the new
electoral system.

Data sources

The election data in this briefing has been compiled from statistical returns
from each Constituency Returning Officer and data published by the
Electoral Management Board. Sources of data relating to previous Senedd
elections (1999-2021) are referenced at the end of this briefing.

This briefing uses the valid vote to calculate and report on turnout. The valid
vote consists of all papers included in the count, including postal ballots but
excluding ballot papers rejected at the count.

Note on diversity information

Data about the characteristics of Members of the Senedd and candidates at
Senedd elections is not routinely collected and is therefore not included in this
briefing.

In March 2025, the previous Welsh Government published diversity and
inclusion guidance for political parties, which included guidance about

not been any data published by political parties. This briefing will be updated if

2. Summary of election results
This section provides a high-level summary of the election
results.
Summary of results

Figure 1 shows the number of seats won by each political party at the Senedd
election. Plaid Cymru are the largest party with 43 seats, followed by Reform UK
with 34 seats. In third place are Welsh Labour with 9 seats, the Welsh
Conservatives came fourth with 7 seats, Wales Green Party came fifth with 2
seats and the Welsh Liberal Democrats came sixth with 1 seat.

New rules for the Seventh Senedd mean that a political party needs to have at
least five Members of the Senedd to form a political group. Political groups have
access to more opportunities for scrutiny (e.g. to ask questions in Plenary and
hold opposition debates) and receive additional resources to support their work.
In the Seventh Senedd, the following political parties were able form a political
group: Plaid Cymru, Reform UK, Welsh Labour and the Welsh Conservatives.

Proportionality of the result

This election used a new electoral system – closed list proportional
representation – for the first time. One of the rationales for introducing this
system was that it would produce a more proportional result than the
previous mixed-member system that was used for elections between 1999-2021.
Proportionality means how closely the percentage of the vote that a party
receives is reflected in their share of seats won.

Table 2 shows how each of the six largest political parties’ national share of the
vote compares to their share of the seats in the Seventh Senedd.

The Gallagher index of disproportionality is used in political science to
measure the disproportionality of an electoral outcome. The lower the Gallagher
figure, the more proportional the outcome. The index was used by the Expert
Panel on Assembly to assess different possible electoral systems for the Senedd.

The 2026 Senedd election was slightly more proportional than the previous
election in 2021. In fact, other than the first election in 1999, it was the most
‘proportional’ election in Senedd history. However, the 2026 election’s score of
9.5, compared to the 2021 election’s score of 10.1 does not suggest a substantial
improvement on proportionality under the new electoral system. 1

New and returning Members of the Senedd

Fifteen Members of the Sixth Senedd did not stand for re-election. Of the 45
who stood again, 29 were re-elected. There were 66 people elected to the
Senedd for the first time. In addition to this, there is one Member (Nerys Evans
MS between 2007-11) who served in a previous Senedd term.

Elin Jones MS and Lynne Neagle MS are the longest serving Members re-
elected. They have both been Members of the Senedd from the establishment
of the institution in 1999. Table 3 shows the MSs elected in 2026 by the Senedd
term they were first elected.

Turnout

Over 2.43 million people were registered to vote at the time of the 2026 Senedd
election. Of these, over 1.25 million people turned out to vote. Based on valid
votes, turnout at this election was 51.7%. This compares to 46.6% at the 2021
election.

The 2026 election saw the highest turnout since the Senedd was established,
achieving over 50% for the first time. Since the 2021 election, a larger group of
people are eligible to vote in Senedd elections, including those aged 16 and 17,
as well as qualifying foreign and EU nationals. Figure 4 compares turnout in
Senedd elections since 1999.

Lost deposits

In order for a political party or an individual candidate to stand at the Senedd
election, a deposit must be paid. Deposits were first introduced for UK
Parliament elections in 1918 to combat concerns that there were “frivolous”
candidates standing for election. The purpose of the deposit was to discourage
candidates from standing who have little chance of winning.

For the Senedd election the deposit for political parties was £500 for the first
candidate appearing on their list in a constituency and £200 for each of the next
five candidates appearing on the list. This means a maximum deposit of £1,500
for political parties in each Senedd constituency. For individual candidates,
the deposit was £500.

Invalid votes

A ballot paper for the Senedd election must be rejected if:

      •   it does not bear the official mark (not the unique identifying mark);

      •   more than one vote is given;

      •   anything is written or marked by which the voter can be identified
          (except the printed ballot paper number or other unique identifying
          mark); or

      •   it is unmarked or void for uncertainty

A total of 2,986 votes cast the Senedd election were rejected as invalid at the
count. This breaks down to 98 for not bearing the official mark, 1,021 for more
than one vote given, 135 for identification of the voter and 1,732 for unmarked or
uncertainty.

All 98 ballot papers rejected for not bearing the official mark were in the
Gwynedd Maldwyn constituency.

Ceredigion Penfro saw the highest number of invalid votes, recording 247 in
total. Bangor Conwy Môn had the lowest number, recording 123 in total.
